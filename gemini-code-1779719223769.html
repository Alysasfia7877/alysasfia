SFM: Food Security Mathematics Application

Applying Derivatives to Food Supply Chains

Modeling the post-harvest biomass degradation rate of local Berangan Bananas (Musa acuminata) under tropical ambient conditions ($28^\circ\text{C}$).

The Bioscience Problem

In Malaysia, high ambient temperatures accelerate the respiration and physiological decay of fresh produce. For vulnerable staple fruits like the Berangan banana, this leads to rapid post-harvest biomass and quality loss, directly affecting national food security and market pricing.

By tracking the first derivative $f'(x)$, food logistics managers can determine the exact day the spoilage rate peaks. This empowers supply chains to precisely time cold-room transfers, processing into secondary goods, or immediate retail distribution before catastrophic loss occurs.

Baseline data parameters adapted from: MARDI (Malaysian Agricultural Research and Development Institute) Postharvest Handling Manuals.

Mathematical Modeling

Biomass Quality Status $f(x)$
f(x) = 100 / (1 + e^(0.7 * (x - 5)))
Where $x$ = Days post-harvest, $f(x)$ = Percentage (%) of pristine marketable quality.
Instantaneous Spoilage Velocity $f'(x)$
f'(x) = -70 * e^(0.7 * (x - 5)) / (1 + e^(0.7 * (x - 5)))^2
Calculates the exact rate of quality degradation (% loss per day) at day $x$.
